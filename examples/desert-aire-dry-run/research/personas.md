# Personas — Desert Aire Heating & Cooling (Mesa, AZ)

DRY RUN — fictional client. Phase 1 research deliverable.
Sources: `.site-director/BRIEF.md` (locked objections + conversion rules) + the
operator's client-context folder (offer docs, runbooks). Existing persona material is thin
(sales-side, not end-customer), so these derive from the agency defaults
(homeowner-urgent, property-manager-planned) plus one persona derived directly from the
brief's upsell-fear objection. No web research used (0 credits).

Locked constraints that shape every persona below:
- Primary conversion = **phone call**. Secondary = quote form (name, phone, service, note).
- **No prices on site.** Sell free-quote speed: "quote today, fixed tomorrow."
- Never promise pricing over the phone — reframe as a strength (written in-person quote).
- No discount-brand positioning. AZ ROC #329417 must appear.

## Persona 1 — "No-Cool Right Now" homeowner (homeowner-urgent) — PRIMARY

**Who:** Mesa/Gilbert homeowner, 30–65. AC just quit or is blowing warm air. It is
July, 110°+ outside, house climbing past 90°. Possibly kids, pets, or an elderly
parent in the house. This is the persona the homepage hero must serve first.

**Job-to-be-done:** *Get cold air back today, from someone I can trust not to exploit
the emergency.* Not researching — triaging. Will call the first company that looks
legitimate AND answers.

**Top objections (in their words):**
1. Price uncertainty — "Emergency call in July… they know I'm desperate. What's this
   going to cost me?" (fear of heat-wave gouging / surprise bill)
2. Upsell fear — "They'll tell me the whole unit's shot and quote me five figures
   when it's a $300 part."
3. No-show fear — "If I book them and they don't show, I've lost a day in a 95° house
   and have to start over."

**Decision triggers:**
- Same-day / "today" stated explicitly above the fold — not "fast service," *today*.
- A tappable phone number in the first screen; a human answers (after-hours coverage
  is a known industry failure — hub receptionist runbook: most shops roll to voicemail
  after 5pm).
- Concrete arrival promise: time window + "we text you when the tech is on the way."
- Legitimacy at a glance: AZ ROC #329417, "family-run, 14 years in Mesa," review stars.
- "Free quote, in writing, before any work" — kills the gouging fear without showing prices.

**Device + urgency:** Mobile, ~always. Standing in a hot house searching
"ac repair mesa" / "ac not cooling." Urgency: maximum — decision inside 5 minutes,
first credible answerer wins. Design implication: click-to-call sticky on mobile,
zero friction before the number, trust markers visible without scrolling.

**Messaging angle:** Lead with relief and speed, grounded in heat-country empathy —
"We know what 115° feels like. If your AC quit, we can usually be there today." Then
immediately disarm the emergency-pricing fear: you'll get a written quote before any
work starts, and the number you approve is the number you pay — no phone quotes,
no surprises, we'll shoot you straight. Short sentences, verbs, no corporate filler.
Every section for this persona ends at the phone number; the quote form is the
fallback for people who can't talk right now (at work, on hold with the home warranty).

## Persona 2 — "Repair or Replace?" homeowner (homeowner-planned)

**Who:** Homeowner with a 10–18-year-old system that limps along — weak cooling,
rising APS/SRP bills, a repair last summer. Not in crisis today, but knows the next
110° week could be the one. Often already burned by a competitor's replacement pitch.

**Job-to-be-done:** *Get a straight answer on whether my system needs a fix or a
replacement — from someone with no incentive to lie to me — and a plan before peak
summer.* This persona is comparison-shopping 2–3 companies.

**Top objections (in their words):**
1. Upsell fear (dominant) — "Every company I call says I need a new unit. Nobody
   will just fix it." This objection is *why this persona exists*; it's the brief's
   #2 locked objection and the hardest to answer with copy alone.
2. Price uncertainty — "I can't get a straight number out of anyone; I don't want a
   sales ambush in my living room."
3. No-show fear (mild) — more "will they respect my time" than emergency stakes.

**Decision triggers:**
- A stated repair-first stance: "If it can be fixed, we fix it. If it can't, we show
  you why — parts in hand." Nobody else in the market says this plainly.
- Free quote with zero-pressure framing ("quote today, fixed tomorrow" reads as
  speed *and* no-obligation).
- 14 years family-run + ROC license + reviews that mention honesty ("didn't try to
  sell me a new unit").
- Educational tone on the Services page: what a repair visit involves, honest signs a
  system actually is at end-of-life. Candor is the differentiator; discounting is
  off-brand and off-limits.

**Device + urgency:** Mixed — evening couch research on mobile, follow-up comparison
on desktop. Urgency: medium, self-imposed deadline of "before it gets really hot."
Reads 2–3 screens deep; will actually visit /services. Converts via quote form as
often as by phone.

**Messaging angle:** Lead with candor, not equipment — "We'll shoot you straight:
most of the systems we look at can be repaired." Position Desert Aire as the second
opinion that ends the runaround: a free, written, no-pressure quote from a family
company that's been in Mesa 14 years and has nothing to gain from selling you a unit
you don't need. If replacement genuinely is the right call, we show our work and let
you decide on your own schedule. Warm, plainspoken, zero sales-brochure gloss —
this persona's trust is won in the sentences the competitors won't write.

## Persona 3 — East Valley property manager (property-manager-planned) — SECONDARY

**Who:** Manages 10–80 doors (single-family rentals, small multifamily) across Mesa,
Gilbert, Chandler, Tempe. Every summer AC failure is a tenant-habitability clock
(Arizona: cooling is an essential service) plus an angry-tenant clock. Wants a bench
vendor, not a one-off hero.

**Job-to-be-done:** *Find a reliable HVAC vendor I can dispatch without babysitting —
answers the phone, shows up when they say, documents everything — so tenant cooling
emergencies stop landing on my desk twice.*

**Top objections (in their words):**
1. No-show fear (dominant) — "A no-show for me is a tenant escalation, a re-dispatch,
   and my name on the complaint." Reliability outranks price.
2. Price uncertainty — needs predictable, documented quotes owners will approve;
   surprise invoices burn the owner relationship.
3. Upsell fear — "Techs pitch replacements to tenants who can't authorize them, and
   I inherit the mess." Wants findings reported to *them*, not sold on-site.

**Decision triggers:**
- Response-time commitment in writing; same-day capability across the whole service
  area (Mesa + Gilbert + Chandler + Tempe listed explicitly).
- Written quote before work + emailed documentation — approval paper trail is the product.
- AZ ROC #329417 stated plainly (vendor-compliance checkbox; unlicensed = disqualified).
- 14 years in business (stability signal: will still exist next summer).
- A quote form that respects the "service + note" fields — they'd rather write two
  sentences with a property address than repeat themselves on a call.

**Device + urgency:** Desktop during business hours, mobile when a tenant emergency
hits at 7pm. Urgency: planned/recurring with urgent spikes. Bookmarks vendors; the
/contact page and form matter more than the hero.

**Messaging angle:** Speak to the second dispatch they don't want to make — "When a
tenant calls you about a dead AC, you need it handled once." Desert Aire answers,
shows up in the window we give you, and sends a written quote and job summary you
can forward to the owner — no on-site upselling to tenants, findings come to you.
Licensed (ROC #329417), family-run, 14 years across the East Valley. Businesslike
warmth: fewer exclamation points than homeowner copy, same straight-shooter voice.
Give this persona a one-line nod on Home and a real block on /services and /contact.

## Objection → proof mapping

The copywriter builds trust blocks from this table. Proof elements available from the
brief: **license** (AZ ROC #329417), **longevity** (family-run, 14 years, Mesa local),
**reviews** (block placeholder — fictional client, no real corpus), **response time**
(same-day positioning), **quote promise** ("quote today, fixed tomorrow" + written
quote before work). Items marked *proposed* are pledge language derived from the
locked voice profile ("we'll shoot you straight") — in a real engagement, confirm
wording with the client before publishing.

| Objection (locked in brief) | The fear underneath | Trust element that answers it | Trust-block copy direction | Must appear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price uncertainty | "They'll exploit my emergency / ambush me with a bill" | **Quote promise** + license | "Free quote, in writing, before we touch anything. The number you approve is the number you pay. That's why we don't quote prices over the phone — we'd rather be right than fast-talk you." Turns the no-phone-pricing rule into proof of honesty. Never pair with discount language. | Hero subline; beside every call CTA; /services intro; form header |
| Upsell fear ("new unit when a repair would do") | "Their incentive is to sell, not to fix" | **Repair-first pledge** (*proposed*) + longevity + reviews | "If it can be fixed, we fix it. If it can't, we show you the failed part and the math — then you decide." Back with "family-run, 14 years in Mesa — we're still here because we don't burn neighbors," and review block themed on honesty. | Dedicated Home trust block; top of /services; near replacement-related copy |
| No-show / late techs | "I'll waste a day in a hot house / face a tenant escalation" | **Response time** + reviews | "Same-day service in the East Valley. You get an arrival window and a text when your tech is on the way." Review block themed on punctuality. Form confirmation microcopy: "We'll call you back today." | Hero (the "today" claim); /contact; quote-form success state |
| (Baseline, unstated) "Is this company even legit?" | "Anyone with a truck can say HVAC" | **License** + longevity | "Licensed, bonded AZ ROC #329417 · Family-run in Mesa for 14 years." Compliance line, not a headline — quiet and everywhere. Satisfies PM vendor screening. | Site footer (every page); near primary CTA on Home; /contact |

**Priority note for the copywriter:** Persona 1 gets the hero and the phone number.
Persona 2 gets the repair-first trust block and /services depth. Persona 3 gets the
quote form, the documentation promise, and the license line. All three read the same
straight-shooter voice — the personas change *which proof leads*, never the tone.
